# Scientific novelty and overlap review — PDU submission candidate

## Earlier paper by the same author

Husain, W., “F-mode oscillations of neutron stars with dark matter from neutron decay: Implications for gravitational-wave detectability,” *Physics of the Dark Universe* **51** (2026) 102208, DOI: 10.1016/j.dark.2025.102208.

The earlier published work studies neutron-decay dark matter with self-interactions, QMC-based stellar structure, f-mode frequencies and damping, universal relations, characteristic strain and prospects for the Einstein Telescope. These are **not new contributions** of the current paper.

## Present manuscript: defensible new elements

1. Audits the original four-fixed-EoS machine-learning benchmark, including leakage and train–test-group issues; retracts its 97% generalization interpretation.
2. Creates population-level synthetic EoS families, validates observations at the EoS level, and reports a class-balanced 340-EoS primary benchmark that passed a full-interior numerical causality audit.
3. Quantifies how family variation and measurement errors cause nucleonic and neutron-decay dark-matter-admixed classifications to overlap.
4. Compares detectability conditional on a known nuclear EoS against classification when the nuclear EoS is unknown.
5. Reports calibrations, independent hyperparameter baselines, alternative parametrization sensitivity and a transparent diagnostic of uncertainty.

## Claims that should NOT appear

- This is the first calculation of f-mode shifts caused by neutron-decay dark matter.
- We have discovered a new dark-sector mechanism or derived a microscopic dark-matter abundance.
- We have independently solved a general two-fluid dark-matter eigenmode problem.
- The numerical 63.1% and 36.7% values are universal bounds on the information content of gravitational waves.
- Every model in the 340-EoS primary set is viable under **both** full-interior causality **and** the narrower dark-fermion mass-stability window. The mass-window restriction has a separately reported sensitivity test, not a joint-filtered primary analysis.

## Remaining scientifically material risks

- The main 340-EoS sample is fully screened for causality but the neutron-decay dark-matter mass prior includes the exploratory region above the stable neutron-dark-fermion window. A simultaneously causality- and particle-stability-filtered **primary** analysis would be stronger for PDU.
- Four families are phenomenological proxies; no systematic microphysical hybrid-star population or hyperon-fraction inference.
- The primary 340-EoS results include two learners and two scenarios; the six-learner, calibration, bootstrap and other extended metrics use the original 360-EoS set, which included nine noncausal models.
- The main dark-matter f-mode eigenproblem uses an effective single-fluid response. Realistic two-fluid perturbations and transport may change discriminatory power.
- The prior published PDU article and the new manuscript must remain explicitly distinguished in the introduction and cover letter.
