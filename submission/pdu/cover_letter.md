Subject: New Full-Length Article submission to Physics of the Dark Universe

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript entitled **“Identifiability Limits of Neutron-Star Matter Composition: Equation-of-State Uncertainty, Dark Matter and Machine-Learning Classification”** for publication as a Full Length Article in *Physics of the Dark Universe*.

The manuscript addresses a specific challenge for gravitational-wave probes of dark matter in neutron stars: a predicted change in the fundamental oscillation mode does not, by itself, establish that a dark component could be distinguished from uncertainty in the nuclear equation of state (EoS). We evaluate this question using general-relativistic stellar calculations, EoS-level held-out classification and observationally motivated uncertainty scenarios.

The causality-audited primary study includes 340 phenomenological EoSs, balanced across four composition-inspired families. Ensemble classification accuracy is 63.1% without observational error and 36.7% with NICER-like mass–radius errors and a 20% f-mode frequency uncertainty, versus a 25% four-class chance baseline. Nucleonic and neutron-decay dark-matter-admixed models remain strongly degenerate in the specified populations. In a separate paired nuclear-versus-admixed calculation, the median f-mode shift is approximately 0.56%, illustrating why uncertainty in the nuclear baseline matters.

**Relationship to previously published work.** My paper “F-mode oscillations of neutron stars with dark matter from neutron decay: Implications for gravitational-wave detectability,” *Physics of the Dark Universe* 51 (2026) 102208, DOI: 10.1016/j.dark.2025.102208, studied stellar structure, the effects of dark self-interactions, oscillation trends and prospective gravitational-wave detectability for selected QMC-based models. The present manuscript does not claim those calculations or the neutron-decay dark-matter mechanism as new. Instead, it investigates whether dark-sector signatures remain statistically identifiable across variable EoS populations, unseen-EoS validation and illustrative observational errors. It also reports an explicit audit of a previously overoptimistic classification result.

The work is deliberately limited in scope: the EoS families are phenomenological and not a population of independent microscopic hyperonic, hybrid or two-fluid dark-matter oscillation models. The manuscript distinguishes its primary fully causality-screened results from secondary comparisons, and it does not claim constraints on dark-matter abundance or a universal detectability bound.

An earlier version of this investigation is publicly available as arXiv:2512.23758. The submitted study has been substantially reconstructed, and the earlier benchmark's conclusions are explicitly corrected. Source code, numerical inputs and verification scripts are available in the associated research repository.
